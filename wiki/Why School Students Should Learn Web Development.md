> This document is written for any school student who has wondered whether learning web development is worth their time — and what it could realistically do for them.

The short answer is: web development lets you build things that real people use — and get paid for it while you are still in school.

This document covers what web development actually is, the concrete opportunities it opens for you at the school level, the competitions you can enter, and what the financial reality looks like both locally in Sri Lanka and globally.

---

## What Web Development Actually Is

Web development is the practice of building software that runs in a browser or is delivered over the internet. It splits into three areas:

**Frontend** — everything the user sees and interacts with. Built with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. A frontend developer turns a design into a working interface.

**Backend** — everything that runs on a server. Handles databases, authentication, business logic, and APIs. Built with languages like Node.js, Python, Java, or PHP.

**Full Stack** — a developer who can do both. As a student, this is your highest-value target because it means you can build an entire product alone.

---

## Why Learn It While You Are Still in School

### 1. You Can Build Real Things Immediately

Unlike most school subjects, web development has zero gap between learning and producing. On day one of HTML you can build a page that runs in a browser. By month three you can build a system that your school can actually use. The feedback loop is immediate and visible — that is rare in any discipline.

### 2. Your Portfolio Can Start Before University

University admissions and scholarship panels, especially for IT-related fields, respond to evidence. A GitHub repository containing a school management system you built in Grade 11 is more convincing than any certificate. You are not describing capability — you are demonstrating it.

### 3. The Skill Compounds

Every project you build teaches you something the next project benefits from. A student who starts at Grade 8 and writes serious code through Grade 13 enters university with five years of real experience — more than most graduates in the hiring pool.

### 4. It Teaches You to Think Like an Engineer

Web development is not just about typing code. It trains you to structure a project, handle edge cases in real data, design for users who are not you, and ship something that actually works in production. These are software engineering skills — and they are in demand everywhere, regardless of what programming language you know.

## Competitions — Local (Sri Lanka)

### Devthon — UOM Leo Club, University of Moratuwa

The most directly relevant competition for a school student who builds web applications. Devthon is Sri Lanka's largest dedicated web development competition, and it has an explicit **School Category**. Teams are challenged to build a functional web or mobile solution to a real-world problem.

- **What you build:** A working prototype addressing a real problem (education, health, agriculture, civic services, etc.)
- **What you demonstrate:** Full-stack thinking, UI/UX decisions, database design, and your ability to present technical work to a panel
- **Why it matters:** Winning or placing in the school category at a university-run competition is strong evidence for university applications and scholarship panels

**Deadline watch:** School Category deadlines typically fall in January. Follow [uomleos.org](https://devthon.uomleos.org/) for announcements.

---

### SLIIT Codefest — Sri Lanka Institute of Information Technology

Now in its 15th consecutive year (Codefest 2026), this is one of the largest national ICT competitions in Sri Lanka, organized in collaboration with the Ministry of Education. It runs a **school-level track** covering software application design and development island-wide.

- **What you build:** A software application (web, desktop, or mobile) addressing a defined theme
- **What makes it strong for you:** Ministry of Education backing means the competition is recognized at the national level — results carry weight in the A/L university admissions process
- Reach: [codefest.lk](https://codefest.lk/)

---

### BestWeb.LK — LK Domain Registry

Organized annually since 2009, this is Sri Lanka's only dedicated **web design competition**, now in its 16th year. It judges live `.lk` websites (not prototypes) across 24 categories — including education. Awards are Gold, Silver, Bronze, Best Mobile Optimized, and Most Popular Website.

- **What this means for a student:** If you deploy a real web project (for your school, your community, a club) on a `.lk` domain, you are eligible to enter
- **Why it is different:** This is not a hackathon. It evaluates real, deployed work — which means the bar is higher, but the credential is also more substantial
- Reach: [bestweb.lk](https://bestweb.lk/)

---

### MOE National ICT Championship — Ministry of Education

A national-level ICT competition directly run by the Ministry of Education for school students. It is open island-wide and includes categories covering software and web development projects.

- **Why it matters:** Recognition from the Ministry of Education carries official weight in the Sri Lankan academic system
- Register through: [moe.gov.lk](https://moe.gov.lk/en/competition/ict/)

---

### Young Computer Scientist (YCS) — FITIS

Run by the Federation of Information Technology Industry Sri Lanka (FITIS). A national platform to submit a full-stack project — a school management system, a library system, a civic tool — for formal technical evaluation.

- **What panels evaluate:** Functionality, technical depth, documentation quality, and your ability to present and defend your design decisions
- Strong for projects that solve a real institutional problem — a school system, a community tool, a local service directory

---

### Mora UX — University of Moratuwa (UX Design Track)

A design-focused competition where frontend and UI/UX skill is the primary criterion. If you are strong in HTML, CSS, and user interface thinking, this is the arena.

- **What judges look for:** User research, wireframing, prototyping, and whether the interface actually solves a user's problem rather than just looking good
- Good preparation: Build real interfaces, get real feedback from real users, and document what you changed and why

## Competitions — International

### NASA Space Apps Challenge — Global Hackathon

One of the world's largest hackathons. In 2025 it attracted over 114,000 participants across 551 events in 167 countries. It is a two-day event where teams build open-source solutions to challenges written by NASA subject matter experts, using NASA's open data.

- **Open to school students** — all ages and skill levels are explicitly welcome
- **Categories include** web development, data visualization, AI tooling, and storytelling
- **A Sri Lanka local event is typically hosted** — you do not need to travel internationally to participate
- The 2025 theme was _Learn, Launch, Lead_; each year brings new challenge categories
- Reach: [spaceappschallenge.org](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/)

---

### Apple Swift Student Challenge

Apple directly organizes this competition for students to build an interactive app using Swift (Apple's programming language). 350 winners are selected globally, and 50 Distinguished Winners are invited to the Apple Campus in Cupertino, California.

- Requires learning Swift/SwiftUI rather than web technologies — but the underlying skills (UI thinking, data modeling, accessibility) transfer directly
- A Distinguished Winner invitation to Cupertino at school age is a career-defining credential

---

### MEGA Hackathon — International Student Competition

An annual hackathon structured around the United Nations' Sustainable Development Goals. It includes a web development category, is open to students from all backgrounds, and explicitly requires no prerequisites other than interest and willingness to build.

- Teams address global problems (health, education, clean energy, infrastructure) using web applications, machine learning, or data science tools
- Reach: [devpost.com](https://devpost.com/)

## Career Paths and Financial Reality

### Employment — Local Sri Lankan Market (2026 Estimates)

Salaries in the Sri Lankan IT sector have shifted significantly due to demand for remote-ready talent. Figures below are for junior to mid-level developers at local companies, in LKR per month:

| Role                 | Junior (0–2 yrs)      | Mid-Level (3–5 yrs)   |
| -------------------- | --------------------- | --------------------- |
| Frontend Developer   | LKR 100,000 – 180,000 | LKR 250,000 – 450,000 |
| Backend Developer    | LKR 110,000 – 200,000 | LKR 300,000 – 550,000 |
| Full Stack Developer | LKR 130,000 – 230,000 | LKR 350,000 – 650,000 |

Backend roles carry a 10–15% premium because they involve database architecture and server security — areas companies treat as high-risk responsibilities. A developer who can do both front and back is the most cost-effective hire for a small or mid-sized company.

**Note on remote work:** Many Sri Lankan developers do not work for local companies at all. They take remote positions with UK, US, or Australian companies and are paid in foreign currency. A mid-level Full Stack developer on a remote contract can earn USD 2,000–5,000 per month — figures that are entirely out of reach in traditional career paths available to school leavers.

---

### Freelancing — Working for Yourself

Freelancing is where web development pays the most for a student, because you can take on an entire project alone rather than being hired for a narrow role.

**Hourly rates (global market):**

| Level                       | USD per hour | LKR equivalent       |
| --------------------------- | ------------ | -------------------- |
| Junior (1–2 yrs experience) | $15 – $30    | LKR 4,500 – 9,000    |
| Intermediate                | $30 – $60    | LKR 9,000 – 18,000   |
| Expert                      | $60 – $120+  | LKR 18,000 – 36,000+ |

**Project-based rates (local and regional market):**

| Project type                                         | LKR range            |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Simple landing page / portfolio                      | 50,000 – 120,000     |
| Business website (5–10 pages)                        | 80,000 – 200,000     |
| Professional e-commerce site                         | 150,000 – 400,000    |
| Custom management system (school, clinic, inventory) | 450,000 – 1,200,000+ |

The last row is the most important for a student who has already built something like a library or school management system. A client hiring for that kind of project normally needs to pay three people separately: a UI designer, a frontend developer, and a backend developer. A full-stack student who can do all three gets a higher single fee — and the experience of running a real project from requirements to delivery.

Platforms where students find freelance work: **Fiverr**, **Upwork**, **Toptal** (senior level), **PeoplePerHour**, and increasingly through direct LinkedIn outreach to small businesses.

### Entrepreneurship — Building Your Own Product

This is the highest-ceiling path, and it is the one most school students do not consider because it feels distant. It is not.

A web developer can ship a product with zero capital: free tiers of hosting (Vercel, Railway, Supabase), free domain trials, open-source tools for everything. The cost of starting is a laptop and time. For comparison, starting a physical business requires inventory, rent, and equipment before you make a single rupee.

**What school students have built that became real businesses:**

- SaaS tools (subscription software for a niche — scheduling, invoicing, inventory)
- Directories and marketplaces (connecting service providers with customers locally)
- Educational platforms (tutoring, notes, practice tools)
- Agency work (building websites for local businesses, then building a team)

The competitive advantage of starting young is compounding time. A student who builds and ships a product at 16 has a completely different skill base at 22 than someone who waits until university to begin.

## The Full Stack Advantage — A Summary

The single biggest ROI for a school-level developer is becoming full stack. Here is the practical reason:

A client with a project — say, a small business that needs an inventory and billing system — has two options:

1. Hire a designer ($X) + a frontend developer ($Y) + a backend developer ($Z) + coordinate between all three
2. Hire one full-stack developer for $(X + Y + Z) × 0.7 and get a single point of contact who owns the entire product

Option 2 wins almost every time for small and medium projects. That single developer is you. The premium paid to a full-stack developer is not a small bonus — it is structural, because you are replacing multiple roles.

## A Realistic Roadmap for a School Student

This is not a commitment — it is a reference. Adapt it to your own pace.

| Phase       | What to focus on                                             | What you can enter or ship                           |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Months 1–3  | HTML, CSS fundamentals, basic JavaScript                     | Personal portfolio site                              |
| Months 4–6  | DOM manipulation, responsive design, Git                     | Enter a local school hackathon                       |
| Months 7–12 | Node.js or a backend framework, databases, REST APIs         | Build a real tool for your school                    |
| Year 2      | Full-stack project from scratch, deployment, version control | Enter Devthon (School Category), SLIIT Codefest      |
| Year 3+     | Security fundamentals, auth, open source contributions       | Freelance first clients, NASA Space Apps, BestWeb.LK |

---

## The Bottom Line

Web development is one of the very few skills you can learn at school age and immediately use to produce things that matter — a real site, a real tool, a real product. You do not need to wait for a degree, a job offer, or anyone's permission.

The students who start early are the ones who enter university already employable, already having shipped real software, and already having a body of work that no exam result alone can match.

Web development is not a distraction from serious computer science. It is the proof that you can do it.
