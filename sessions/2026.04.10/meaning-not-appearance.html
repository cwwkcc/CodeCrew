HTML Is About Meaning — Not How Things Look

1. What the br Tag Actually Does

Without br — this is one paragraph, the browser wraps it naturally:

Line one. Line two. Line three.

With br — line breaks are forced inside the same paragraph:

Line one.
Line two.
Line three.

Wrong use of br — using it to fake paragraph spacing:

This is my first idea.

This is my second idea.

This is my first idea.

This is my second idea.

The gap above was made with two br tags. This is wrong. If content is a new paragraph, use a new p tag — not br. br is only for line breaks within a single block of content.

Correct use of br

a postal address:

CodeCrew
Beruwala
Kalutara District
Sri Lanka

poem or song lyrics:

Twinkle, twinkle, little star
How I wonder what you are

2. Tags Define Meaning — CSS Controls Appearance

Look at the two lines below. They look the same. But in the DOM, they are completely different things.

This is how normal p tag looks

I am an h1 tag — styled to look like a paragraph.

This is how normal h1 tag looks

I am a p tag — styled to look like a heading.

Now look at the two lines below. They also look the same. But again — completely different in meaning.

I am strong — I am important content.

I am b — I just look bold. I carry no meaning.

3. Why This Matters

A browser renders both the same way on screen. But these tools read the actual tags — not the visual output:

  • Screen readers — announce h1 as the main heading of the page. They skip over a p tag, even if it looks bold and big.
  • Search engines — treat h1 content as the most important text on the page for ranking. A styled p tag gets no such weight.
  • Browser reading mode — strips all CSS and relies purely on tags to build the readable version of the page.
  • Other developers — read your HTML to understand the structure of your page, not to admire how it looks.

The purpose of an HTML tag is to tell the browser and the world what your content means — not what it should look like. CSS is a separate job.

Always ask: what is this content? Then pick the tag that matches that meaning.
